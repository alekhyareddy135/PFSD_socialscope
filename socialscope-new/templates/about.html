About This Project

Methodology & References

An advanced research platform combining NLP, MongoDB, and peer-reviewed research to examine social media's dual impact on children and adolescents.

Project methodology
  • Systematic literature review of peer-reviewed studies published 2015–2024 on social media and adolescent wellbeing.
  • Analysis of longitudinal datasets: Millennium Cohort Study (UK), Monitoring the Future (USA), HBSC international survey.
  • Case studies selected for large sample sizes (n > 1,000), pre-registered methodologies, and independent replication.
  • NLP engine uses a curated 150+ word lexicon with weighted scoring, negation handling, and sqrt-dampened normalisation.
  • Interactive model algorithm derived from effect sizes in Twenge & Campbell (2019) and Przybylski & Weinstein (2017).
  • Recommendations cross-referenced with UK Online Safety Act (2023), EU DSA (2024), and UNICEF digital rights guidance.
  • MongoDB schema designed for scalability — all API endpoints are swap-ready for full Atlas integration.

Social media is a tool. Outcomes depend on how it is used.

Neither panic nor dismissal serves children well. The evidence calls for nuanced, evidence-based responses from every stakeholder — matched to the specific age, platform, and context of each child's digital life.
